## Observed Trends (2025–2026)

1. **From single medical LLMs to medical agents.** The field moved from exam-style QA models (Med-PaLM line) to agentic systems that plan, call tools, and act inside clinical environments (TxAgent, RiskAgent, MedRAX, DoctorAgent-RL).
2. **Realistic agent benchmarks.** Evaluation is shifting from MedQA-style exams toward interactive, task-based environments: MedAgentBench (FHIR virtual EHR), HealthBench (physician-rubric conversations), ChestAgentBench. Static QA accuracy is no longer treated as sufficient evidence of clinical readiness.
3. **Agentic RAG.** Retrieval is being folded into the agent loop (query planning, iterative retrieval, self-critique) rather than used as a one-shot pre-generation step (Agentic RAG survey; guideline-grounded RAG validated across 10 LLMs in npj Digital Medicine).
4. **Memory as a first-class module.** Dedicated surveys now taxonomize agent memory construction, management, and retrieval, but longitudinal *patient*-anchored memory remains largely unexplored.
5. **Standardized agent infrastructure.** MCP, A2A, ACP, and ANP protocols are emerging as the interoperability layer for multi-agent systems, including healthcare deployments.
6. **Safety, trust, and regulation moved to the foreground.** Medical hallucination taxonomies, uncertainty quantification, multi-agent safety stress-testing (MedSentry), trustworthy-agent threat surveys, and regulatory analyses (unregulated device-like output; UNDCS regulation proposals) all appeared within twelve months.
7. **Prospective clinical deployment.** A small number of systems crossed from retrospective benchmarks into prospective bedside use (COMPOSER-LLM sepsis prediction), setting a higher evidence bar for clinical AI agents.
8. **Human-AI collaboration evidence is mixed.** Meta-analysis of clinician-LLM collaboration shows suggestive but uncertain gains with non-trivial error rates, reinforcing the need for structured human-in-the-loop design rather than ad-hoc oversight.
